And Coffee!
This may seem an unexpected pairing, but wine and coffee are natural companions — and wine lovers appreciate exceptional coffee. Robert’s journey began in the 1970s, making wine in his Berkeley garage in what was then — and still is — known as the Gourmet Ghetto. This North Berkeley enclave birthed California Cuisine, a movement that transformed American food culture. Chez Panisse became the nation’s first true farm-to-table gourmet restaurant. The Cheese Board revolutionized artisanal cheese retail. Au Chocolate pioneered the American chocolate truffle. And Peet’s Coffee introduced Americans to single-source, richly dark-roasted coffee. It was a cultural happening that rippled across the country — you can credit Peet’s for inspiring the coffee culture that Starbucks later popularized, since the founder of Starbucks trained under Alfred Peet himself. Mr. Peet was a friend and mentor to Robert; a stern Dutchman with an unparalleled gift for selecting and roasting coffee. Robert and his family have been devoted to his craft since he opened his roastery in 1966.
Gifted with an exceptional palate, Robert created his own blend — the Rex Blend. Coffee blending mirrors winemaking for good reason: just as grapes from different regions and varietals express distinct characteristics, so too does coffee from different plantations and regions around the world. A thoughtfully crafted blend can be far superior to any single-source coffee. Then comes the roasting — a deceptively complex art. Time, temperature and movement all play critical roles in achieving the perfect cup.
Recently, Cecilia and Robert found themselves in the lab, working on wine blends over cups of exceptional coffee. She’s developed her own signature blend — Ceci’s Blend. As we collaborated, we asked ourselves: why keep these discoveries to ourselves? Together, we’ve decided to honor both our passions and share them with you.
Wine club members will receive samples of both blends — Rex Blend and Ceci’s Blend — with their wine allocation shipments. Both will be whole bean; there’s simply no other way to experience coffee properly. If you don’t already own a grinder, I urge you to invest in one — freshly ground coffee makes an incomparable difference. I store my beans in the freezer between refilling my grinder to maintain optimal freshness. We’re still adding the coffee offerings to the website, so stay tuned.
Consider this our initial foray into coffee. We’re excited to see where this goes.
Thank you for your continued support.
